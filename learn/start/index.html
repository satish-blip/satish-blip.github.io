Quick start

Four steps to get the most out of the lessons.

  1. Choose where to begin. New to the subject: start at Step 1 (maths). Comfortable with probability and matrices: start at Step 2 (traditional ML). Know the classic algorithms: start at Step 3 (neural networks). Each card below says who it suits.
  2. Read the plain-words opening. Each lesson starts with what the method does and why, before any formula. A dotted word is a glossary term: click it for a short definition and a link to its full explanation.
  3. Play with every lab. Change the data, drag the points and move the sliders; the plot and the numbers beside it update at once. Use Watch it run to step through an algorithm line by line.
  4. Practise in Play. When a lesson clicks, try the method on real data in the ML Playground: its panels link back to the lessons, so the two work together.

The learning path

The steps are in learning order: each builds on the ones before. Slide sideways to see all ten; each card opens that step.

Step 1

Maths foundations

Probability and statistics (Bayes, maximum likelihood, inference), linear algebra (vectors, matrices, decompositions) and calculus and optimisation.

Start here if the formulas in ML papers look unfamiliar. Step 2

Traditional ML

Linear and logistic regression, trees, ensembles, SVMs, PCA; then unsupervised learning, time series, evaluating models, explaining models and applied ML.

Start here if you know the maths but not the algorithms. Step 3

Neural networks

The multilayer perceptron, activations, optimisers, residual connections and normalisation: how networks learn and go deep.

Start here if you know classic ML. Step 4

Recurrent networks

Networks with memory for sequences: the RNN through time, and the LSTM and GRU gates.

Needs Step 3. Step 5

NLP & transformers

Tokens and embeddings, attention in all its forms, the transformer, retrieval-augmented generation, prompting, LLM pre-training and post-training, and AI agents.

Needs Steps 3 and 4. Step 6

Vision & multimodal

Convolutions and CNNs, detection and segmentation, images as tokens and models that read images and text together, then generative models, self-supervised learning and audio.

Needs Steps 3 and 5. Step 7

Efficient deep learning

Making trained models small and fast: quantisation, pruning, distillation and efficient architectures.

Needs Steps 3 to 6. Step 8

Reinforcement learning

Learning to act from rewards: Markov decision processes, Bellman equations and bandits, then Q-learning, DQN, policy gradients and PPO.

Needs Steps 1 and 3. Step 9

Graph neural networks

Learning from connections: graphs as matrices, message passing, graph convolution and attention.

Needs Steps 1 and 3. Step 10

MLOps & responsible AI

Serving, feature stores, drift monitoring and safe releases; then fairness, robustness, security and privacy.

For anyone shipping a model.

How a lesson works

Every lesson page has the same parts, in the same order. Open one to see the details.

1Plain-words opening

What the method does, when to use it and the intuition, with no jargon taken for granted. The maths comes after, and every plot sits beside the explanation it illustrates.

2Glossary pop-ups

Technical words are links with a dotted underline. Click one for a short definition and a link to the section that explains it fully, so you can fill a gap without losing your place.

3Interactive labs

Plots you can change: pick a data set, drag points, move sliders. Controls sit under the plot and the numbers update live. On a wide screen the plot stays in view while you read the text beside it.

4“Watch it run” animations

Algorithms step by step next to their pseudocode: play, pause, step forward or back, drag the slider, change the speed (0.25× to 2×) or click a line of pseudocode to jump to it.

5Evolution timelines

Each step opens with a timeline from the first idea to today’s state of the art, so you see why each method was invented.

6Python and credits

Most lessons end with the Python to do the same thing with standard libraries, and credit the books and guides they draw on.

7Check yourself

Some lessons have short quizzes with the answer explained, so you can test what stuck before moving on.

8The sidebar and “On this page”

On a wide screen the left sidebar is the whole learning path with a Find a topic box; the right panel lists the sections of the current page with previous and next lessons. Both fold away to give the lesson more room.

9Text level, theme and your place

The Text setting in the sidebar switches between Verbose and Clean (Clean folds the long hints). Pages remember where you were, so coming back puts you at the same spot.

Questions

Short answers to what people ask most.

How much maths do I need?

Step 1 covers everything the later steps use, from scratch. If a later page uses a term you do not know, its glossary pop-up links back to the maths section that explains it.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Every lab and model runs in your browser. The Python at the end of a lesson is for when you want to repeat it on your own computer.

In what order should I read?

The steps are in order, and so are the lessons inside each step. Skipping ahead is fine: each card above says what it needs.

The page looks out of date after an update.

Hard-refresh: Ctrl + Shift + R on Windows and Linux, Cmd + Shift + R on a Mac.
