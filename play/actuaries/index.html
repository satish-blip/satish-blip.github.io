Play · Actuaries

Actuarial models you can touch.

Four playgrounds for insurance work, each built from the Casualty Actuarial Society’s monograph series and checked against its worked examples or against Python: price with a GLM, set reserves, fit loss distributions and build a capital model. They share one layout and one set of views, and they run entirely in your browser.

The four tools, in the order the work flows

Distributions feed pricing and capital; reserves become a line in the capital model.

Step 1 · Monograph 2

Loss Distributions

How big and how many claims: severity fits with deductibles and limits, layers and increased limits factors, claim counts, the aggregate loss by FFT, extreme-value tails and credibility.

Try first: the Danish fire losses, then the Q–Q plot of the fits.

Open Distributions

Step 2 · Monograph 5

GLM Pricing Studio

The rating model: Poisson, gamma and Tweedie GLMs with exposure, relativities with intervals, lift, double lift and Gini, and the statsmodels code.

Try first: the motor portfolio, Claim frequency, Fit GLM.

Open GLM pricing

Step 3 · Monographs 3, 4

Reserving Playground

What is still to pay: chain ladder, Bornhuetter–Ferguson, Cape Cod, Mack, the ODP GLM and bootstrap, and one-year risk.

Try first: the Taylor & Ashe triangle, then Mack.

Open Reserving

Step 4 · Monographs 15, 12

Capital Modelling

The whole company: lines and reserve risk joined by copulas, reinsurance, VaR and TVaR capital, Euler allocation and distortion pricing.

Try first: switch the copula to Clayton and watch the allocation.

Open Capital

Four views of every tool

Switch at the top of any tool. The same views exist in the ML & DL tools.

Tabs

The numbered stages one at a time, each panel with a ? for every setting, a Learn link and </> Code with the Python.

Workflow

The stages as boxes on a canvas. Click a box to change its settings; optional steps (Mack, reinsurance, allocation) can be dropped in or taken out.

Statistical

Every formula behind the result, written out with your numbers: the likelihood with truncation and censoring, the GLM, Mack’s variance, VaR, TVaR, Euler and distortion pricing.

Dependency

How each input drives the answer: factors to the price, development factors to the reserve, frequency and severity to the aggregate, dependence and retention to the capital. With what-if controls.

3

The CAS monographs, one at a time

Choose a monograph to see what it covers and where it lives on this site. Summaries are in my own words.

No. 2Built here

Distributions for Actuaries

David Bahnemann

What it covers. Parametric severity and frequency distributions fitted to claim data: limited expected values, increased limits factors, deductibles and layers, trend leveraging, truncation and censoring, and the aggregate distribution.

On this site. Loss Distributions: every stage follows its notation, with section numbers beside the panels.

No. 3Built here

Stochastic Loss Reserving Using Generalized Linear Models

Greg Taylor and Gráinne McGuire

What it covers. The chain ladder written as a GLM (the over-dispersed Poisson model), and the GLM extensions that let a reserving model handle what the chain ladder cannot.

On this site. Reserving: the ODP GLM stage, and the gamma, Gaussian and inverse Gaussian GLMs under Advanced.

No. 4Built here

Using the ODP Bootstrap Model: A Practitioner’s Guide

Mark Shapland

What it covers. How to bootstrap the over-dispersed Poisson model in practice: residuals, the degrees-of-freedom adjustment, non-constant scale, diagnostics and the distribution of the reserve.

On this site. Reserving: the ODP & bootstrap stage, with residual plots by origin, development and calendar year.

No. 5Built here

Generalized Linear Models for Insurance Rating (2nd edition)

Mark Goldburd, Anand Khare, Dan Tevet and Dmitriy Guller

What it covers. The end-to-end guide to rating with GLMs: families and links, offsets and weights, factor types, base levels, validation by lift, double lift and Gini, and model refinement.

On this site. GLM Pricing Studio: its charts and checks, with section numbers beside each panel.

No. 12Built here

The Actuary and Enterprise Risk Management: Integrating Reserve Variability

Mark R. Shapland and Jeffrey A. Courchene

What it covers. How reserve-variability results feed into enterprise risk management, worked through a case study.

On this site. Capital Modelling: reserve risk is one of the lines of business, correlated with the others; the distribution comes from the Reserving methods.

No. 13Built here

Penalized Regression and Lasso Credibility

What it covers. Using lasso and other penalties so that a GLM-style model shrinks thin segments towards the overall level, which is credibility in another form.

On this site. Loss Distributions, Credibility models stage (credibility as a ridge or lasso penalty), and the penalties in the GLM Pricing Studio.

No. 14Built here

Practical Mixed Models for Actuaries

Ernesto Schirmacher

What it covers. Linear and generalised linear mixed models and how their random effects reproduce Bühlmann–Straub credibility.

On this site. Loss Distributions, Credibility models stage: the same shrinkage as a mixed model.

No. 15Built here

Introduction to Capital Modeling and Portfolio Management

John A. Major and Stephen J. Mildenhall

What it covers. Building a property-casualty capital model and using it: risk measures, capital adequacy, reinsurance, capital allocation, and pricing by distortion with its natural allocation.

On this site. Capital Modelling: every stage, with chapter references beside the panels.

No. 16Partly / related

From GLMs to Comprehensive Insurance Pricing: Techniques and Challenges

Alan Chalk, David Deacon, Montserrat Guillen and Max Martinelli

What it covers. The challenges of turning models into a rating plan, with a framework for pricing that stays transparent and reliable.

On this site. Partly: the GLM side is in the GLM Pricing Studio; the wider pricing framework is not built yet.

No. 6Partly / related

A Machine-Learning Approach to Parameter Estimation

Jim Kunce and Som Chatterjee

What it covers. Using machine learning to estimate distribution parameters from data, instead of splitting the data into cells.

On this site. Related: the tree and boosting models in the ML Playground. A tool for this approach is not built yet.

No. 9Partly / related

Data Quality Management in the P&C Insurance Sector

Graham Hall, Mark Jones, Kevin Madigan and Steve Zheng

What it covers. Data quality for insurers: the ideas, the regulation, frameworks and how to measure it.

On this site. Related: the data health checks in the ML Playground (missing values, duplicates, outliers, leakage).

No. 8Planned

Stochastic Loss Reserving Using Bayesian MCMC Models (2nd edition)

Glenn Meyers

What it covers. Bayesian reserving models fitted by MCMC, tested against Mack and the ODP bootstrap on many real triangles, plus a risk-margin approach. It replaces Monograph 1.

On this site. Not built yet. Today the Reserving tool has Mack and the ODP bootstrap it is compared with.

No. 10Planned

Using the Hayne MLE Models: A Practitioner’s Guide

Mark Shapland

What it covers. Practical use of Hayne’s maximum-likelihood framework for distributions of unpaid claims.

On this site. Not built yet.

No. 11Not a modelling topic

Federal Income Taxes: Provisions Affecting Property and Casualty Insurers

Arlene M. Richardson and Joel S. Chansky

What it covers. How US federal tax law, including the 2017 changes, affects an insurer’s after-tax income and investments.

On this site. Not planned: it is about tax rules rather than models.

No. 17Not a modelling topic

Professionalism

Taub

What it covers. Actuarial professionalism.

On this site. Not planned: it is about professional standards rather than models.

Also in development at the CAS, and on the list here once published: machine-learning regression for ratemaking, game theory, and extreme value theory (the Distributions tool already has a peaks-over-threshold stage).
