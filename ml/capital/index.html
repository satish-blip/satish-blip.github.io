From lines of business to capital and prices

An insurer holds capital so that it can pay claims in a bad year. A capital model simulates many possible years for every line of business together, measures how bad the bad years are, and then asks which lines use the capital and what they should be charged for it. Every panel has a </> Code button with the same calculation in Python, and every setting a ? that explains it.

  1. Lines of business. Each line’s claim count and claim size, turned into the distribution of its yearly total by FFT. Three sample books, including one with reserve risk.
  2. Dependence. A copula (Gaussian, t, Clayton, Gumbel) joins the lines; see how often their bad years come together.
  3. Reinsurance. Per-claim excess of loss and quota share; the capital they save and what that is worth.
  4. Risk & capital. VaR, TVaR, the capital they call for and the expected policyholder deficit.
  5. Allocate capital. Stand-alone, covariance, co-TVaR and co-VaR; the diversification benefit and each line’s return on its capital.
  6. Price the risk. A distortion calibrated to a cost of capital, and its natural allocation of the margin to the lines.
  7. Checks & export. Checks of the engine against exact results, the full Python script and the simulated years as a CSV.

Four views

Tabs shows one stage at a time. Workflow lays the steps out as boxes; add or remove reinsurance, allocation and pricing, and change settings in the side panel. Statistical writes the model out as equations with your numbers. Dependency shows how the size of the book, claim variability, dependence, reinsurance and the confidence level flow into the capital, and how far each one moves it.

Basic and Advanced

Basic covers the lines, dependence, reinsurance, capital, allocation and pricing. Advanced adds the t and comonotonic copulas, co-VaR, the number of simulated years and the seed, and every distortion side by side, each marked with a purple Advanced tag.
