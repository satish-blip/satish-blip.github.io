Loss Distributions Playground

The engine is written from scratch in JavaScript and checked against scipy and the R package actuar (see Checks against scipy and actuar on the last stage). Real data from the CASdatasets R package (Dutang & Charpentier) and actuar. Nothing leaves your browser.

Code

From claim amounts to prices and tails

An actuary prices cover by describing losses with a probability distribution: how often they happen and how big they are. The stages go from the raw losses to a fitted distribution, then use it for deductibles, limits, the year’s total and the extreme tail. Every panel has a </> Code button with the same calculation in Python (scipy), and every setting a ? that explains it.

  1. Claims data. Real losses (Danish fire, US liability with policy limits, Secura Re large losses) or a synthetic set with known truth hidden behind deductibles and limits. Look at the log-scale histogram first.
  2. Fit severity. Seven families fitted by maximum likelihood, allowing for deductibles (truncation) and limits (censoring), ranked by AIC, with the curves, a Q–Q plot and (Advanced) standard errors and goodness of fit.
  3. Limits & layers. What a deductible saves, what a higher limit costs (increased limits factors), and how inflation leverages through a layer.
  4. Frequency. Claim counts per policy with exposure: Poisson, negative binomial and binomial, and the (a, b, 0) check.
  5. Aggregate loss. Frequency and severity together: the distribution of the year’s total by FFT, VaR, TVaR and stop-loss premiums.
  6. Extreme values. A generalised Pareto above a threshold, with mean excess and Hill plots to choose it, and tail VaR and expected shortfall.
  7. Credibility models. Bühlmann–Straub: how far to trust a small group’s own experience, and the same shrinkage as a mixed model or a ridge or lasso penalty.
  8. Checks & export. All fits side by side, the checks against scipy and actuar, a CSV of the fits and the full Python script.

Four views

Tabs shows one stage at a time. Workflow lays the steps out as boxes on a canvas; add or remove the optional ones and change settings in the side panel. Statistical writes the model out as equations with your numbers. Dependency traces how the frequency, severity parameters, deductible, limit and trend flow into the yearly cost of the layer, and how far each one can move it.

Basic and Advanced

Basic covers the fit, layers, frequency and the aggregate. Advanced adds, each marked with a purple Advanced tag: standard errors and KS / Anderson–Darling statistics, method-of-moments fits for comparison, a simulation check of the aggregate, and the penalised-regression (ridge and lasso) view of credibility.

Sources

Adapted from: David Bahnemann, Distributions for Actuaries, CAS Monograph No. 2 (Casualty Actuarial Society, 2015): the families, limited expected values, increased limits factors, trend leveraging, truncation and censoring, frequency models and the aggregate distribution follow its notation; section numbers beside the panels point to it. Also: McNeil (1997) and Embrechts, Klüppelberg & Mikosch (1997) for peaks over threshold; Bühlmann & Straub (1970) and Hachemeister (1975) for credibility; CAS Monograph 13 (penalised regression and lasso credibility) and CAS Monograph 14 (Schirmacher, Practical Mixed Models for Actuaries) for the link between credibility, mixed models and penalties.

Data: the CASdatasets R package by Christophe Dutang and Arthur Charpentier (GPL-2 or later): danishuni (Copenhagen Re, as used by McNeil 1997), lossalaefull (ISO general liability, Frees & Valdez 1998), besecura (Secura Re, Beirlant et al. 2004) and norauto; and the actuar R package by Dutang, Goulet and Pigeon (hachemeister).
